# License policy

The requested policy is free use, no software resale, no closed derivatives, public source for distributed/deployed modifications and a contribution-back obligation. This repository therefore uses the custom **FontLab 8 MCP Community Source License 1.0**, not a renamed GPL/AGPL or an OSI-approved open-source license.

The binding text is LICENSE. The owner should obtain legal review before publication, including review of copyright ownership, dependency compatibility, interoperability material and the practical wording of the contribution requirement. This document does not claim enforceability in every jurisdiction.

## Practical intent

- You can use the unmodified tool without payment, including to make commercial fonts.
- Your inputs, fonts, reports and other outputs are not relicensed by using the tool.
- Independent proprietary FontLab installations and MCP clients can communicate with it.
- You cannot sell the adapter, put its code into a closed derivative or turn it into a paid software offering.
- Redistribution and operational use of modifications require public source under the same license; submit the modifications upstream within 30 days. Private experiments/tests can remain private until deployment or redistribution. Acceptance of a PR is not required.
- Independent font work, training and support are distinguished from selling access to the software.
- Third-party components keep their own licenses. Contributions remain owned by their authors under the same project terms.

## Why a custom license

[GPL-family licenses permit selling copies](https://www.gnu.org/licenses/gpl-faq.html#DoesTheGPLAllowMoney); they do not force every fork to submit a PR upstream. A no-sale restriction is outside the [Open Source Definition](https://opensource.org/osd). [PolyForm Noncommercial](https://polyformproject.org/licenses/noncommercial/1.0.0) limits commercial purposes but does not by itself express this project's complete source-sharing/upstream-contribution policy. Creative Commons [recommends software licenses instead of CC licenses for software](https://creativecommons.org/faq/).

Do not market this project as OSI open source. If a standard license becomes preferable, the repository owner and all relevant copyright holders must authorize a compatible change; accepting contributions does not automatically give one person unrestricted relicensing rights.
